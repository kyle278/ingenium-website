# Ingenium launch plan: websites, CRM, and connected websites + CRM

26 September 2026 • Decision plan and campaign brief • All recommended prices in EUR, excluding VAT

## The decision

Launch **one brand with three buying routes**, led by **Ingenium Connected**: a website and CRM delivered together, with the agreed website-to-CRM connection included. Keep website-only and CRM-only offers genuinely independent. They reach buyers at different stages and create future expansion opportunities without forcing a bundle.

Start with established Irish service businesses, particularly construction/home improvement and professional or specialist services. Concentrate on businesses selling quotes or considered purchases, where an enquiry passes through several people or steps. Add a small ecommerce offer for established retailers with usable product data; do not make it a fourth full launch campaign yet.

Use **an implementation fee plus monthly service** as the default. Test transparent weekly price framing, but do not rebuild the business around cheap, cancellable weekly subscriptions. The research does not establish that weekly billing improves profitable retention for B2B website or CRM services.

This is a recommended operating plan, not a claim about Ingenium's present pricing or achieved results. It assumes Ireland first, a maximum €1,500/month cash marketing envelope, about ten founder marketing/sales hours per week, and at most three simultaneous implementations. Geography, budget, capacity, costs and willingness to pay remain assumptions to validate. No advertising, outreach or website changes have been executed.

## 1. What the research means for Ingenium

### Compete on a demonstrated working process

Ingenium already presents connected websites, CRM and automation. That is a useful starting position, but it needs a concrete buyer explanation: **who receives an enquiry, what context they receive, and what happens next**. The current website makes broader platform and AI claims; the launch should put the basic customer journey first. [Ingenium](https://www.ingeniumconsulting.net/)

Connected website-and-CRM products and implementation partners already exist. “Native” alone is therefore not a defensible uniqueness claim. Make the competitive offer a combination of clear implementation scope, visible end-to-end delivery, local accountability, useful training, and explicit ownership and exit terms. The competitor report separates software-only prices from agency delivery costs so a cheap software licence is not mistaken for a complete implementation.

## 2. Three clear offers

| Offer | Buyer and trigger | Promise | First conversion action | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ingenium Websites** | Owner-led firm with a dated site, unclear services or weak proof | Make it easier to understand, trust and contact the business | Book a 20-minute website enquiry review | Real service-page before/after and a tested enquiry path |
| **Ingenium CRM** | Owner/sales/operations lead managing enquiries and quotes across inboxes or spreadsheets | Give each opportunity a visible owner, stage and next action | Book a 30-minute follow-up review | A realistic opportunity progressing through the working system |
| **Ingenium Connected** | Firm rebuilding its website while improving sales operations | Deliver the website and CRM around the same enquiry process | See an enquiry become a next step | End-to-end demonstration, including failure handling and handover |

The proposed staff ranges are approximately 2–20 for websites, 5–50 for CRM and 5–30 for Connected. Workflow complexity, sale economics and an accountable client owner matter more than headcount. Start with two vertical clusters and test actual demand rather than targeting every SME.

Use this route selector on the website and in discovery:

- “Our website needs work” → Websites.
- “Our enquiries and follow-up need organising” → CRM.
- “We need both to work together” → Connected.
- “We need to sell products online” → commerce discovery.
- “We are not sure” → a short diagnostic, then the smallest useful project.

Keep AI as an optional demonstrated capability. Do not let it obscure what the client is buying. Do not force CRM replacement where configuring the incumbent system would solve the problem.

### Define exactly what each starter includes

**Website Launch:** up to five agreed pages, one language, one standard enquiry form, a repeatable design/build approach adapted to the brand, mobile layouts, light editing of client-supplied copy, technical SEO basics, agreed measurement and one handover session. Two consolidated review rounds. Monthly service includes hosting/basic maintenance and up to one hour of small changes. Original brand strategy, extensive copywriting, photography, ecommerce and custom applications are separately scoped.

**CRM Launch:** configure an existing Ingenium capability set for one team, up to five users, one pipeline, agreed standard fields, one clean CSV import up to 2,000 records, up to three simple rules once production acceptance is complete, one agreed report/dashboard, and two training sessions. Monthly service includes a defined platform allowance and up to two support/configuration hours. This is not a ground-up CRM build. External CRM licences, difficult data cleaning, historic attachments, unsupported integrations and new modules are extra. If the included functionality is not release-ready, defer the package or quote only what can be delivered.

**Connected Launch:** Website Launch plus CRM Launch, with the specified native form-to-record connection, agreed source/service context and handoff checks included. Monthly service includes up to three care hours across the combined system. Price and scope cover the standard connection; extra systems require their own scope. The monthly price is €49 below purchasing the two proposed standalone service plans together, or €588 over 12 months. That is an internal package comparison, not a claim about competitors or guaranteed customer savings.

**Commerce Launch:** a standard supported storefront with up to 50 simple products supplied in usable format, one currency, one agreed payment provider, straightforward shipping configuration, policy-page placement, test purchase/refund and handover. Confirm taxes and commercial policies with the merchant. Up to 1.5 monthly care hours. Platform subscriptions, paid apps, gateway fees, product photography, complex variants, ERP/stock synchronisation and multi-market work are excluded. A product catalogue or enquiry site is not evidence of completed ecommerce checkout delivery.

### Commercial structure

1. For standard implementations, propose 50% at kickoff and 50% at acceptance. Monthly service begins at go-live. Define acceptance, client response deadlines and out-of-scope changes in the proposal.
2. For uncertain CRM/custom requirements, start with **€750 paid discovery**, credited against an accepted implementation within 30 days. Deliver a process map, data/integration inventory, acceptance criteria and a fixed scope or explicit estimate. Count discovery labour in project economics when crediting it.
3. Separate finite implementation from continuing hosting/platform/care. The default fully funded build can use monthly rolling service with a proposed 30-day notice period; price forecasts must still account for churn.
4. Offer staged build payments only when the cost model supports them. A narrow website alternative is **€499 upfront + €249/month for 12 months, then €149/month**. The first-year total is €3,487, compared with €3,288 on the standard option. State the €199 difference, the 12-payment obligation, and the post-year-one price. Do not call it cancel-anytime.
5. Do not launch zero-upfront CRM or Connected subscriptions before measuring delivery and collections. Their early cash exposure is much greater.
6. Monthly allowances cover small changes, not unlimited work; unused hours do not roll over. Proposed additional work: agreed estimate at €95/hour, or a new fixed scope. Treat warranty defect fixes separately from the client's change allowance. Define business-hours response expectations without promising instant resolution.
7. Put domain ownership, bespoke asset rights, platform licensing, export format and exit assistance in writing. Recommended principle: the client controls its domain and business data; agreed bespoke website assets transfer after full payment, while Ingenium retains its reusable platform IP. Verify the actual hosting/export path before promising portability. Recurring cancellation ends the included hosting/platform service; it must not silently destroy export rights or erase an outstanding build balance.

### Margin gates

For genuinely custom work, use discovery rather than stretching the starter: internal budgeting hypotheses are €3,000–€8,000+ for complex websites, €6,000–€15,000+ for custom CRM and €8,000–€20,000+ for more extensive connected projects. These are scope-dependent proposal ranges, not researched market averages or promises to deliver every project within them.

The pricing report models loaded delivery labour at €45/hour and modest acquisition costs. It is illustrative, not Ingenium's accounting. Under its assumptions, Connected at €3,900 setup was too exposed to an hours overrun; €4,500 is the stronger launch hypothesis.

Before accepting any starter, require at least **35% first-year contribution after acquisition**, at least **25% under a 25% build-and-support hours overrun**, and approximately **45% recurring contribution before acquisition/fixed overhead**. These are proposed management thresholds. Include platform costs, refunds, payment fees, training, support and real sales time. If costs break the threshold, raise the price, reduce scope or decline the package; do not assume future upsells will repair it.

## 4. Weekly versus monthly: what to do

There are four separate choices: the number shown in marketing, payment frequency, total price and cancellation commitment. A smaller weekly number does not make the service cheaper.

A publisher case reports 20% more subscriptions with weekly framing, but it also changed monthly billing to four-weekly billing and increased annual price. It therefore does not isolate a pure framing effect, and it is not evidence about B2B agency retention. Longer-term retention was still to be measured. [First-person INMA case](https://www.inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hamburger-abendblatt-unlocked-a-20-subscription-uplift-with-weekly-price-framing)

Recommended public presentation:

> **Website Launch: €1,500 implementation + €149/month.** Monthly service is approximately €34.38 per week, billed monthly. €3,288 for implementation and 12 service months, excluding VAT and agreed extras.

Keep implementation and monthly charges prominent. Test whether adding the weekly equivalent helps buyers understand affordability. Do not substitute “from €34/week” for the complete offer.

For arithmetic context, €49/week is €2,548 over 52 payments, or €212.33 per average month; €199/month is €2,388/year. A four-week billing interval normally produces 13 cycles per 52 weeks, not 12. Calendar-year charge counts can differ with billing dates; contract schedules must be explicit.

**Test in stages:** first interview buyers using otherwise identical proposals; then randomly assign website visitors or eligible accounts to monthly-first versus monthly-plus-weekly-equivalent copy. Keep scope, annual total, setup fee, billing, commitment and traffic mix constant. Assign the same account consistently, record the variant and predefine the outcome. Use qualified opportunities and eventual contribution per visitor/account, with 90-day payment/retention guardrails. Do not call a winner after a few sales. At this launch scale, research and objection logs will likely be more useful than a statistically conclusive conversion test. Only test real weekly billing later as a separate experiment, preserving annual price and commitment.

## 5. The three launch campaigns

The following copy is draft material for use once its stated delivery capabilities and offers are confirmed. Full ad, post and email variants are in the [campaign research](launch-lanes-research.md).

### A. Websites — “Make your next enquiry easier”

**Hero:** A website that makes it easier to choose your business.

**Body:** Show what you do, give customers reasons to trust you, and make the next step clear. Ingenium builds websites around the questions your customers need answered before they enquire.

**Offer:** Website Launch, €1,500 implementation + €149/month. Clear page scope, two review rounds, tested enquiries and ongoing care.

**CTA:** Book a website enquiry review.

**Proof asset:** a 60–90 second walkthrough of one real service page: old buyer problem, what changed, and the new enquiry path. Use descriptive evidence until a measured commercial result is available.

**Distribution:** existing relationships and referrals, founder LinkedIn, local partner introductions, then tightly targeted search for website design/rebuild intent. Target the chosen service verticals through examples and copy, not just location keywords.

**Opening campaign post:** “Can someone understand your service, see evidence and request the right next step in one visit? Our website enquiry review looks at those three things and gives you three practical fixes. If your website is due a rethink, start there.”

**Landing-page order:** buyer outcome → approved work → what is included → delivery/client responsibilities → clear price → ownership/care FAQ → review booking. Explicitly say that CRM is optional.

### B. CRM — “Know who owns the next step”

**Hero:** Every opportunity needs an owner and a next step.

**Body:** Bring enquiries, quotes and follow-up into a process your team can use. Ingenium maps the workflow, configures the right system and helps your team put it to work.

**Offer:** CRM Launch, €3,000 implementation + €249/month for the defined starter scope. Complex migrations/custom development begin with paid discovery.

**CTA:** Book a follow-up review.

**Proof asset:** take one synthetic enquiry through qualification, quote, next action and a management view. Show real user actions and state which automation is live. Later collect adoption evidence, such as the share of active opportunities with a recorded next action.

**Distribution:** IT support and accountancy referrals, existing clients, founder-led workflow demonstrations, and a small CRM implementation search pilot. Avoid generic “CRM software” traffic dominated by buyers seeking free self-service tools. Do not target a named software platform unless Ingenium can actually implement it.

**Opening campaign post:** “Look at your last ten enquiries. Can everyone see who owns each one and what happens next? That is where an Ingenium CRM project starts: the working process, then the software.”

**Landing-page order:** recognisable workflow problem → live demonstration → configuration/migration/training scope → fit criteria → pricing and exclusions → implementation responsibilities → review booking. Make clear that the current website can stay.

### C. Connected — “From enquiry to next action”

**Hero:** Your website and CRM, built to work together from day one.

**Body:** Ingenium designs the website and the follow-up process together. The agreed forms, customer records and handoff work as one scoped project, with the standard website-to-CRM connection included.

**Offer:** Connected Launch, €4,500 implementation + €349/month. Extra systems and migration are agreed separately.

**CTA:** See an enquiry become a next step.

**Proof asset:** one uninterrupted three-minute demonstration: submit an enquiry; see its record and source; identify its owner and next action; inspect the pipeline. Include duplicate handling and a failed-submission recovery example in the longer sales demo. Do not promise automatic actions until production acceptance passes.

**Distribution:** promote as the flagship through founder demonstrations, partner workshops, existing website clients and relevant web/CRM buying journeys. Do not depend on high search demand for the exact phrase “native website CRM”.

**Opening campaign post:** “A website launch should include what happens after someone presses Send. Ingenium Connected brings the website and CRM into one project, with the agreed lead flow included. See the handoff before you decide.”

**Landing-page order:** concrete end-to-end outcome → demonstration → standard included connection → approved proof → package/total cost → extra-system exclusions → data/export/support explanation → walkthrough booking.

### Launch incentive and follow-up

Include a **30-day review**, funded within the stated project/support allowances: website enquiry review, CRM adoption review or Connected workflow review. Do not add an uncosted bonus or permanent discount. Offer a limited number of pilot slots only after reserving real delivery capacity.

For people who have opted into launch marketing, use a four-message lane-specific sequence: day 0 outcome and offer; day 3 proof/demo; day 7 scope/ownership/common objection; day 12 invitation to a review. Stop or change the sequence when they book, decline or opt out. A sales response to a requested review is distinct from subscription to ongoing marketing.

Do not treat a publicly listed work email as blanket permission for a campaign. Establish the recipient basis and suppression process before electronic outreach; use requested introductions and opt-in audiences as the launch default. [Irish DPC guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/rules-electronic-and-direct-marketing)

### Be honest about acquisition economics

A launch that closes three projects is not automatically profitable. At the full €4,500 cash ceiling plus 130 founder hours over 13 weeks at an assumed €45/hour, total economic launch cost is **€10,350**. That is €3,450 per win at three wins, or €1,725 at six. Some proof production is reusable investment, but report both fully loaded and marginal acquisition cost; do not erase founder time to make ads look viable.

For an illustrative paid-search scenario, €2,250 at €5/click buys 450 clicks. At 3% enquiry conversion, 60% qualification and 25% qualified-opportunity close, this produces about **2 wins** and roughly **€1,111 paid-media CAC**, before labour and other costs. These are arithmetic assumptions, not Irish CPC or conversion benchmarks. Doubling CPC doubles CAC if other rates stay constant. The model illustrates why referrals and careful qualification should lead the launch.

Set allowable acquisition cost for each offer as: expected 12-month revenue minus delivery/service/fee costs minus required contribution. Derive allowable cost per qualified opportunity by multiplying that CAC by the expected close rate. Review each €250 tranche against observed fit and pipeline; do not spend multiple target CACs just to obtain a qualified lead. Scale only after recorded sales and delivery economics support it. If spending the ceiling is unaffordable, use warm/referral activity and reserve paid budget until there is evidence.

## 7. Sales, measurement and capacity

Use one pipeline with a lane field: enquiry → qualified review → discovery complete → proposal → signed/paid → delivery → live → 30-day review. Keep disqualified/lost reasons separate. An enquiry or booked call alone is not a qualified opportunity.

Qualification must establish a real problem, a process/content owner, viable scope, likely budget and timing. Ask which systems must stay, how data will move, who will approve the project and what acceptance means. Ask for the buyer's own sales value and time-loss estimates before discussing ROI.

The day-90 **learning target**, not forecast, is 24 completed reviews, 12 qualified opportunities, six scoped proposals and three signed projects. If these include one of each main starter, total implementation bookings are €9,000 and monthly service at full activation is €747. With 12 service months each, their combined value is €17,964. Bookings, annualised service and actual collected cash are different measures; dates and churn determine realised revenue.

Track weekly by lane and source:

- Enquiries, attended reviews, qualified opportunities, proposals, wins and lost reasons.
- Cash collected, recurring service activated, fully loaded CAC and expected/actual contribution.
- Estimated versus actual build hours, recurring support hours and payment failures.
- For client delivery: accepted enquiry path; CRM owner/next-action completeness; capture-to-first-human-action time where relevant.

Capture campaign/source, lane, account, qualification, variant where relevant and permission status. Define a single conversion event per actual action and deduplicate repeated submissions. Reconcile five synthetic test enquiries through the website, CRM and reporting before ads. Treat consent-dependent attribution gaps as gaps, not as proof of direct traffic.

Reserve implementation slots by hours, not just project count. The pricing model assumes approximately 20 build hours for Website, 35 for CRM, 50 for Connected and 40 for Commerce. These are aggressive starter assumptions, including project delivery tasks; validate them against actual work before sales commitments. Three simultaneous projects could still overload one delivery person. At ten Connected clients, the included care alone can use 30 hours/month before incidents and general operations. Keep an explicit support reserve and publish dates only after staffing is checked.

## 8. Pilot evidence and scale decisions

Interview 9–12 buyers before finalising the public offer. Ask what triggered the last project, what alternatives they considered, what made pricing credible, who approved it and where the last process failed. Do not ask only whether they like the proposed idea. Follow with real scoped proposals; willingness to pay is best tested by an actual purchasing decision.

For the first three paid projects, record scope changes, hours, client response delays, usage/adoption and support. Get permission before using names or quotes. Compare a real baseline and observation period before publishing results. Show descriptive work when quantitative evidence is unavailable.

At day 90:

| Decision | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Scale a lane | Buyers understand the offer; signed scopes meet contribution thresholds; delivery and support are within capacity; acquisition has a plausible repeatable route |
| Revise | Buyers want the outcome but resist scope/price, or work overruns; change one major variable and retest |
| Pause paid acquisition | Too few qualified buyers, unaffordable CAC, unreliable attribution or missing proof |
| Add ecommerce campaign | Completed commerce capability/proof, a qualified retailer pipeline and budget independent of the core launch |

Do not promise grant eligibility as a purchase incentive. Grow Digital can support eligible new off-the-shelf software and associated configuration under conditions; bespoke websites and custom software are excluded. Obtain a case-specific LEO decision before representing any component as funded. [Official eligibility](https://www.localenterprise.ie/portal/growdigital/who-is-eligible-/)
