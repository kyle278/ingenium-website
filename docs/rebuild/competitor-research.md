# Ingenium competitor research

Research date: 26 September 2026. Geography: Ireland first, UK comparators, global software substitutes. Prepared from public primary company pages; no sales calls, paid accounts, customer interviews, or independent performance audits. Prices are advertised starting prices, not negotiated quotes. Currency and billing periods are retained; VAT is unspecified unless explicitly noted. “Not published” means not found on the inspected pages, not that the supplier never discloses it. Competitor testimonials and performance claims remain supplier claims.

## What matters for Ingenium

The opportunity is to sell a clearly scoped, implemented customer journey to Irish SMEs: a credible website, a usable sales process, and an accountable delivery partner. The mere combination of website and CRM is already available. HubSpot explicitly sells a common customer-data foundation, HighLevel includes websites and pipelines, and Irish agencies already implement connected systems. Ingenium should demonstrate a better fit for a specific buyer and workflow, with transparent cost and support, rather than claim technical uniqueness. [HubSpot products](https://www.hubspot.com/products), [HighLevel](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing), [Web Journey](https://webjourney.ie/hubspot/hubspot-onboarding-services).

Ingenium's current homepage positions websites, CRM, campaigns and AI together for SMEs and names Carlow Hearing, Kenny Construction and Holland Pianos. However, fresh direct HTTP checks by the lead researcher found the project index empty and all three linked project pages returning 404. Older indexed descriptions are candidate material to restore and verify, not currently working proof or verified revenue outcomes. Launch campaigns need separate entry points for website replacement, CRM adoption and the combined system. [Ingenium](https://www.ingeniumconsulting.net/), [project index](https://www.ingeniumconsulting.net/projects).

## Published offers and scope

### 1. WebCo — the low-cost Irish website anchor

**Published:** Launch €495 outright or €59/month, up to seven pages; Growth €1,495 or €149/month, up to 15 pages; Premium from €2,995 or €299/month. Launch includes copy, forms, analytics and Google Business Profile setup. Monthly includes hosting, updates and support; source code remains with WebCo and cancellation takes the site offline. Outright purchase includes source code. Domain/content remain the customer's. One-off payment is 50% to start and 50% at launch. Monthly has no fixed-term lock-in; care/SEO cancellation uses 30 days' notice. Hosting/care separately starts €45/month. [Official offer and terms](https://webco.ie/).

**Interpretation:** Strong for buyers who mainly want local enquiries and a low outlay. Its visible demo library provides something tangible to inspect; demos are not automatically customer-result evidence. Ingenium should compare workflow scope and operating ownership, not just page count or claims of bespoke code. Website-only pricing cannot be justified by saying merely “fast, custom and SEO-ready.”

### 2. SEOweb — budget builds plus optional retainers

**Published:** Starter from €399 for up to five pages; Growth from €799 for up to ten; WooCommerce ecommerce from €899+. Ecommerce mentions catalogue, payments and training; three months' support is listed for Growth/ecommerce. Care is €79/month; SEO €299/month; content €149/month. Website builds advertise no lock-in. Store catalogue size, migration complexity, VAT treatment and transaction costs are not resolved on the pricing page. [Pricing](https://seoweb.ie/pricing/).

**Interpretation:** Useful lower-bound offer, not evidence of the average price of a fully scoped Irish website or store. Ask what product import, tax/shipping setup, copywriting and ongoing support actually cover before comparing. Ingenium can win on a precise acceptance checklist and a supported post-launch sales process, provided it proves those extras matter to the buyer.

### 3. StackLabs — an Irish website subscription benchmark

**Published:** Monthly billing: Starter €199/month + €299 setup for three months; Growth €169/month + €199 for six months; Partner €149/month + €99 for 12 months. Prepaid equivalents are €169, €143 and €126/month respectively. Scope increases from 5/10/15 pages and 3/4/5 monthly content requests. Hosting, lead capture, analytics and a lead-management system are included; launch is typically 2–4 weeks. Ownership and early-termination mechanics need further confirmation. [Pricing](https://www.stacklabs.ie/pricing).

**Interpretation:** Do not present €126 as a cancel-anytime monthly charge. This is a strong comparator for packaging revisions and updates into a managed offer. “Lead management” is adjacent to CRM, but the inspected page does not establish advanced pipeline, permissions or automation equivalence. Ingenium should demonstrate the actual journey and define included workflows, records and users.

### 4. MyDiro Digital — ownership as a subscription selling point

**Published:** £89.99/month launch offer, normally £129.99, £0 upfront and no VAT added. Initial 12-month term; remaining payments are payable on early exit. Afterwards it becomes monthly with 30 days' notice. The website transfers to the customer after 12 months; domain, content and business data belong to the customer from day one. Two monthly development hours, research and Google Business Profile work are advertised. First collection occurs at launch. [Offer and ownership details](https://mydiro.co.uk/pay-monthly-websites/).

**Interpretation:** This challenges any claim that subscriptions must mean permanent rental. Its page shows research artefacts and links a named client case study, although results were not independently verified here. Ingenium should publish an equally intelligible ownership/exit policy and prove the quality of its discovery output. The temporary launch rate must not be treated as the regular market rate.

### 5. Sitescription — inexpensive managed websites with software cross-sell

**Published:** £199 setup + £99/month; no VAT added. Domain year one, one email mailbox, hosting and up to four small changes monthly are included. One month's cancellation notice. Customer content/data can be exported; the build remains on the managed platform. The pricing page also lists optional extras despite broad all-inclusive wording: Hive CRM £9/month, QuoteKing £49/month, tracking setup £99 once; ecommerce and custom integrations are separately quoted. [Pricing](https://sitescription.com/pricing).

**Interpretation:** An especially useful combined-offer comparator, but a cheap CRM add-on is not proof of a fully implemented native website-to-sales workflow. Its self-service brief/payment funnel is a model for standard website scopes. Ingenium should use a fit check before payment for complex CRM work. Avoid broad “everything included” language that later conflicts with exclusions.

### 6. Web Journey — direct Irish HubSpot and website competitor

**Published:** Guided Sales Hub Pro or Service Hub Pro onboarding from €1,600 each; Enterprise €3,320 each. Guided Marketing Pro €2,830; Enterprise €6,200. Done-for-you technical implementation is scoped separately. Services include HubSpot CMS websites, training and ongoing management. Its mmi Analytics testimonial describes website, sales, service and automation work; the site lists 40 Academy certifications. [Onboarding, scope and pricing](https://webjourney.ie/hubspot/hubspot-onboarding-services).

**Interpretation:** Do not compare a fully implemented Ingenium CRM directly against the guided €1,600 offer without explaining who does the setup. Software fees also need their own line. The competitive lesson is to separate guidance, implementation and ongoing administration. Buyers already committed to HubSpot may reasonably prefer its specialist ecosystem experience.

### 7. Cloudtech — Irish Zoho business-systems consolidation

**Published:** Implements Zoho CRM and Zoho One, presenting linked sales, marketing, support, HR, accounting and operations as an alternative to fragmented systems. Uses a consultation-led sales process. No implementation price, minimum term, migration allowance or quantified customer proof was found on the inspected homepage. [Cloudtech](https://www.cloudtech.ie/).

**Interpretation:** Strong conceptual competitor to “one business system.” It may suit buyers whose problem extends well beyond lead capture into HR or accounting. Ingenium should avoid drifting into a full ERP/operations promise. Win an explicitly smaller first implementation: enquiry capture, accountable follow-up, pipeline visibility and handover. Broader custom systems should be separately discovered and scoped.

### 8. DMIC — implementation and adoption competitor

**Published:** Zoho One/app setup, data migration, integrations, custom fields/workflows/dashboards, staff training, troubleshooting and continuous improvement. No public implementation price or fixed contractual term was found on the inspected service page. [Zoho consulting](https://www.dmic.ie/Zoho-Consulting-Services).

**Interpretation:** Shows that configuration alone is insufficient as a premium claim: other consultancies already sell training and optimisation. Ingenium's CRM offer needs a defined adoption plan, named process owner and real acceptance tests. For an established Zoho customer, improvement of the existing platform can be a better recommendation than replacement; do not force the combined website offer into every opportunity.

### 9. HubGem — UK specialist benchmark for CRM value

**Published:** Education-focused HubSpot onboarding: Essentials from £10,560 + VAT; Enhance £12,533; Elevate £14,126; Enterprise £14,099. Packages differ in support, training and complexity; these are implementation services, not licence prices. The company cites work with 130 educational institutions across 18 countries and specialist partner credentials. [Education onboarding](https://www.hubgem.co.uk/hubspot-onboarding-with-hubgem). Re-onboarding separately starts £4,420 + VAT for an eight-week programme. [Re-onboarding](https://www.hubgem.co.uk/re-onboarding).

**Interpretation:** This is not a like-for-like price anchor for a small local SME pipeline. It demonstrates how sector knowledge, migration risk and adoption depth support higher fees. Ingenium can borrow the specificity: sell the contractor's quote journey or a professional firm's consultation journey, rather than a generic list of CRM features. Sector expertise must be earned and evidenced.

### 10. StudioForty9 — established ecommerce specialist

**Published:** Irish agency covering ecommerce strategy, design, development, integrations and marketing, with over 20 years' experience claimed. Named client testimony includes Intersport Elverys and Petstop. Its delivery process includes scoping, design, build, UAT and launch; it identifies itself as a Shopify Premier Partner. No project price was published on the inspected pages. [Agency and proof](https://www.studioforty9.com/), [delivery](https://www.studioforty9.com/pages/delivery).

**Interpretation:** For larger retail migrations this is a serious specialist, not an overpriced version of a brochure-site provider. Ingenium's launch should target simpler stores with bounded catalogues and fulfilment requirements, or refer/partner on complex ERP, inventory and multi-market work. Require test orders, payment/refund tests and data reconciliation. A commerce site and a lead-generation website are different delivery products.

### 11. HubSpot — software substitute and connected-platform competitor

**Published:** Website content, marketing, sales and service products share Smart CRM; forms can populate contacts and content can use CRM data. [Product architecture](https://www.hubspot.com/products). Free CRM is available; the inspected CRM product page advertises Starter from US$15/month/seat as a new-customer discount, Professional from US$50 and Enterprise from US$75. These are not a quote for every Hub plus website implementation. [CRM product and pricing caveat](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm).

**Interpretation:** The real substitute may be “we can start free,” not another agency. Explain the work Ingenium performs: data design, cleanup, process configuration, migration checks and training. Do not argue that platforms are inherently disconnected or require expensive integrations for every website form. Obtain a current scoped licence quote before cost comparisons involving automation, contacts or seats.

### 12. HighLevel — all-in-one and white-label competitor

**Published:** Starter US$97/month or US$970/year, three subaccounts; Unlimited US$297/month or US$2,970/year; Agency Pro US$497/month or US$4,970/year. Core includes website/funnel builder, CRM pipelines, forms, calendars and workflows. Usage charges apply to some messaging/AI capabilities. Agency Pro adds SaaS resale features. [Official pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing).

**Interpretation:** Cheap agency software access is not zero-cost delivery: setup, quality assurance, support, communications usage and acquisition remain. This is also a competitive threat from agencies selling similar bundles. Ingenium must make clear whether it sells its own platform, a configured third-party platform or a hybrid. Buyers should understand licences, data export and what happens if they leave. Native connection alone will not distinguish the offer.

### 13. Shopify — ecommerce software alternative

**Published:** Ireland Basic €32/month monthly or €24/month annual equivalent; Grow €92/€69; Advanced €384/€289. Plus from €2,100/month. Basic standard online card rate is listed as 2% + €0.25 excluding VAT, with different rates for other payment types. Platform includes store building, inventory, customer segments and marketing functions. [Irish pricing and feature table](https://www.shopify.com/ie/pricing).

**Interpretation:** A prospect can buy ecommerce software cheaply, but still needs catalogue preparation, brand/copy, fulfilment configuration and operational testing. Separate Ingenium's implementation fee from platform, app and processing charges. A proposed connected CRM workflow should solve an additional need—such as high-value sales enquiries or trade-account follow-up—rather than duplicate a store's existing customer records.

## Sales battlecards: how to respond credibly

The responses below are proposed Ingenium positioning, conditional on delivery capability; they are not verified claims about Ingenium's present service levels.

| Buyer objection | Useful response | Proof to show | When to concede |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I found a much cheaper website.” | “Let's compare the agreed scope, ownership and what happens after an enquiry arrives.” | One-page scope and 12-month costs; actual form-to-inbox/pipeline demo | Basic brochure needs with a hard low budget |
| “We can buy CRM software ourselves.” | “You can. Our fee is for setting up your process, preparing the data and helping your team use it.” | Migration reconciliation; role-based training; live sales pipeline | Team has the capacity and expertise to self-implement |
| “Our website already connects to our CRM.” | “Then let's identify whether anything actually breaks before recommending a replacement.” | Journey audit and measured failure points | Existing workflow already meets needs |
| “Why not use a HubSpot or Zoho partner?” | “Compare platform expertise and the exact delivery outcomes you need.” | Relevant implementation references, capability matrix, acceptance criteria | Complex platform work beyond Ingenium's experience |
| “A subscription means I never own anything.” | “The contract should distinguish your domain/data, the website build and any platform licence.” | Written ownership, export and exit example | Required portable codebase is unavailable in Ingenium's model |
| “All-in-one should have no extra costs.” | “The core connection can be included; migration, messaging and external systems need clear allowances.” | Included/excluded matrix and sample invoice | Buyer requires unbounded custom work at a fixed low subscription |
| “Can you handle our entire ecommerce operation?” | “First map catalogue, payments, stock, fulfilment and returns.” | End-to-end order/refund test and integration map | Complex retail transformation exceeds capacity |

## Differentiation and campaign implications

1. **Lead with a specific operational change.** A recommended combined demo is: form submitted → source captured → owner assigned → acknowledgement sent → follow-up task created → opportunity shown in a pipeline. Show the whole journey in 90 seconds. Avoid generic dashboards as the only proof.
2. **Choose two initial service sectors.** Construction/trades and professional service businesses are hypotheses worth testing; existing Ingenium project records provide relevant starting material. Run interviews before claiming a sector preference is validated. Clinics can be explored, but health-data workflows need more care and should not be the generic first demo.
3. **Make website-only a credible standalone product.** Do not make the buyer purchase CRM merely to get a site. Offer a clear upgrade path based on enquiry volume, number of staff and follow-up complexity.
4. **Make CRM-only a workflow implementation.** A diagnostic, fixed initial scope, team training and adoption review form a stronger offer than “custom CRM.” Existing sites should be retained if appropriate.
5. **Make combined pricing explicit.** Phrase the differentiator as “website and CRM connection included in the agreed core scope,” subject to technical verification. Avoid blanket promises of zero integration cost for accounting, legacy CRMs, ecommerce stock or bespoke APIs.
6. **Publish an ownership/exit page.** State domain registration, content rights, customer-data export formats, source-code rights, licence dependencies, cancellation and buyout mechanics. This addresses a real comparison axis visible in competitor offers.
7. **Build evidence before aggressive promises.** Obtain permission for named case studies; record baseline and post-launch qualified enquiries, response time, follow-up completion and appointments. Separate changes delivered from outcomes measured.
8. **Ecommerce remains a scoped variant.** Start with manageable catalogues and uncomplicated shipping/payment requirements. Price complex migration and back-office systems separately.
9. **Avoid competitor attack campaigns initially.** Intent pages such as “website with CRM for Irish service businesses” or a guide comparing ownership and ongoing care fit the evidence better than unsupported “best/cheapest” claims.
10. **Weekly billing is a test, not a competitor conclusion.** This sample mainly exposes monthly, annual and project offers. Their existence says nothing causal about whether weekly collection converts better. A weekly price display and an actual weekly subscription are different experiments; assess acquisition quality, realised margin and retention, not just clicks.

## Gaps to close before public comparison claims

- Ask shortlisted suppliers for comparable scoped quotes if precise savings claims are planned; public entry prices cannot support them.
- Confirm Ingenium's actual deployment architecture, core CRM features, support capacity, delivery hours, portability and third-party recurring costs.
- Verify current contract/VAT details when quoting; pricing pages can change and some promotional rates lack a fixed end date.
- Interview five website-only buyers, five CRM users and five combined-system prospects. Ask what happened to their last ten enquiries and what previous implementations cost in staff time.
- Treat all supplier case studies as self-reported until checked. No review-platform scores, market shares or win rates were established in this research.
- Refresh prices quarterly and capture the scope used in every competitor comparison. No competitor ranking or industry average is implied by this purposive sample.
