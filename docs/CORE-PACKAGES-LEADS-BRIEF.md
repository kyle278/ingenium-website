**Problem type:** Offer/positioning clarity first. Do not declare an advertising failure or sales failure without funnel data.

## 2. Lead magnet (or core offer decision)

- **Decision:** Advertise one relevant core offer to each relevant audience first. Retain the existing Google Visibility Check as a secondary diagnosis for Starter.
- **Narrow problem:** Starter: “Can a referred customer understand and contact us?” Growth: “Where does the last website enquiry go?” CRM: “Where are staff copying the same information?”
- **Type:** Reveal the problem through a short practical review. A deeper CRM process audit is paid discovery when appropriate.
- **Delivery vehicle:** Human conversation plus a short written note. Use existing reusable templates rather than a new software product.
- **Name candidates to test:** “Google Visibility Check”; “Enquiry Journey Check”; “Where Does the Next Enquiry Go?”
- **Consumption plan:** One clear observation, evidence from the customer's current setup, and a useful next step. No invented revenue-loss calculator.
- **CTA:** Starter → request a quote before the actual promotion deadline. Growth → plan the enquiry journey. CRM → map one process. A free conversation is not a promise of a complete unpaid systems audit.

Keep the visibility check below the three core homepage routes. It should support a purchase decision, not become a fourth competing package.

## 4. Playbook

### Warm relationships

**Owner:** Clayton / sales owner, with Kyle supporting technical qualification.
**List:** Existing appropriate customer and professional relationships, past enquiries where follow-up is appropriate, local business introductions and relevant partner referrals. Do not assume every historical contact is a permitted marketing list.

**Daily input:** [assumption: 10 individually relevant relationship conversations or follow-ups on working days]. Scale down if the accessible warm audience is small. This is an adaptation of Hormozi's volume principle, not his literal Rule of 100.

**Starter conversation, used with an appropriate warm contact:**
“Hi [name], I saw [specific real update about their business]. When someone recommends you, where do you send them to check your services? We are offering a defined one-page website with hosting and technical care. If that is useful, I can show you the scope and the exact setup and monthly cost.”

**Starter follow-up when they show interest:**
“The Starter build is €495 until 5 November at 07:02:20 Irish time, then €795. Hosting and technical care are €50 per month from launch. No VAT charged. If you submit the Starter quote request through our site before the deadline, we can confirm whether the scope fits and honour the offer for that eligible request. There is no payment to request a quote.”

**Business Growth conversation:**
“Hi [name], you mentioned [actual difficulty with enquiries or quotes]. How do you currently see which customers need a reply? We have a starting package that connects the website enquiry, a simple customer record and an acknowledgement. The idea is that your team can see what happens next. Would it help to walk through one example from your business?”

**CRM conversation:**
“Hi [name], you mentioned [actual repeated task or handover]. Which information gets copied or chased most often? We can map that process first and show what could be centralised in Ingenium Portal. If the fit is there, you receive a defined implementation scope and ongoing costs before deciding.”

**Referral conversation, only when appropriate:**
“Do you know an owner of a landscaping, cleaning or contracting business who is finding enquiries and quotes hard to keep track of? If someone comes to mind, please ask whether they would like an introduction. We can start with one real example of their current process.”

**Follow-up:** [assumption: one useful follow-up after three working days, and a final appropriate check the following week]. Provide relevant information, not repeated pressure. Stop when asked or when the conversation is not appropriate. Do not automate this cadence without reviewing consent and channel rules.

### Useful content

**Owner:** Kyle provides the practical example; sales/design can adapt it.
**Daily input:** [assumption: one short proof or teaching asset per working day, using up to 60 minutes]. This is a capacity test, not a publishing guarantee.

Content sequence, labelled as proposed:
1. Give: “What should a referred customer find on your website?” Show a real, authorised example or a clearly labelled demo.
2. Give: “Who needs a reply?” Explain a simple pipeline using dummy records.
3. Give: “What an enquiry acknowledgement can and cannot promise.”
4. Give: “Why a CRM does not automatically import all your messages.”

The proposed sequence has four give pieces per direct ask. The content must still stand on its own. Do not invent a client result to make a post persuasive.

Starter content checklist for missing content; pipeline guide for adoption concerns; workflow map for unclear scope. This brief is a writing plan, not authorisation to send messages.

## 6. The math

Use recorded figures:

Contacts appropriately approached × engagement rate = engaged conversations.
Engaged conversations × qualified-call rate = qualified calls.
Calls held × deposit conversion = paid projects.
Collected setup fees − attributable acquisition and delivery cost = setup contribution.

Subscription contribution = collected subscription − hosting − licences − variable email/API/automation cost − support labour.
Observed retained lifetime contribution, plus setup contribution, informs LTGP.

**Do not invent a projected CAC:** The inputs are unavailable.
**Book benchmark:** Hormozi uses a target of at least 3:1 LTGP:CAC. This is a diagnostic reference, not a guarantee or an industry average.
**30-day cash check:** Starter promotional deposit €247.50; standard deposit €397.50. Growth deposit is from €900 at the stated starting scope. CRM initial deposit is from €1,500. These are arithmetic from the user's terms.

## 7. Scale path (More, Better, New)

- **More:** Increase appropriate activity only when there is an available audience, response capacity and delivery margin. Do not double an unqualified list.
- **Better:** [assumption: review one funnel bottleneck weekly]. If people choose a package but do not request a call, investigate trust, pricing and friction. If calls do not turn into deposits, investigate fit, scope, objections and follow-through.
- **New:** Add another relevant placement for proven content before another platform or a paid channel. A paid test needs a capped budget and an affordable CAC derived from contribution.
- **Weekly experiment:** [assumption: test one headline or CTA at a time]. Low-volume results are directional. Do not claim statistical significance without sufficient observations.

## 8. Lead getters

**Referrals:** Ask after a useful, accepted delivery, using the specific segment and problem.
**Employees:** Document the useful conversation, demo and follow-up process before hiring.
**Agencies:** No agency retainer recommended without knowing the offer's economics.
**Affiliates:** No commission structure or programme assumed.

- **Roadmap level today:** Not enough evidence to assign a number honestly.
- **Next level requires:** Reliable package-level conversion, contribution, delivery capacity and real customer proof.

The next hire should address an observed bottleneck, not a theoretical need for a sales team.
