# Ingenium: three-lane launch research and campaign working plan

Prepared 26 September 2026. Research and recommendations only; no campaigns published and no messages sent. Prices are intentionally deferred to the separate pricing workstream. All numeric campaign targets, allocations, package boundaries, and timelines below are proposed operating assumptions, not market benchmarks or existing Ingenium commitments.

## Recommendation

Launch one brand with three clear buying routes. Make the jointly built website + CRM the flagship, website projects the simpler entry route, and CRM projects a diagnostic-led offer for businesses whose existing website can stay. Concentrate the first sales effort on established Irish service businesses selling considered purchases or quotes. Treat ecommerce as a scoped extension, because storefronts, catalogue migration, payment, inventory, returns and customer communications introduce a different delivery workload.

The strongest message is a concrete operational promise: an enquiry arrives with context, has an owner, and has a next step. Prove that path in a live demonstration. Do not lead every campaign with AI, an all-purpose platform, or cheap billing alone.

## Buyer segmentation and routing

Initial geographic assumption: Republic of Ireland, English language, prices in EUR. Start with two vertical clusters: construction/home improvement and professional or specialist services. Specialist retail/service hybrids can enter the website lane. Clinics may fit existing proof, but handling patient information expands requirements; keep initial demonstrations to ordinary enquiry data unless the product has been specifically validated for sensitive workflows.

| Lane | Initial buyer hypothesis | Trigger | Job to be done | Route elsewhere when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Websites | Owner-led business, roughly 2–20 staff; established offer, poor or dated website; person available to approve content | New service, repositioning, weak mobile enquiries, outdated proof, dependence on referrals | Explain the business credibly and make enquiries easier | They only need a temporary page; no content owner; cannot support enquiries |
| CRM | Owner, sales or operations lead; roughly 5–50 staff; multiple people handling quotes or follow-up | Leads in inboxes/spreadsheets, duplicate entry, weak adoption of current CRM | Give every opportunity a reliable owner, stage and next action | A contact list is sufficient; no agreed process owner; incumbent can solve the issue with simple configuration |
| Website + CRM | Established service firm; roughly 5–30 staff; meaningful value per sale, several steps between enquiry and sale | Website rebuild coincides with operational pain or new acquisition campaign | Make capture and follow-up work as one workflow | Enterprise migration, unsupported regulated process, complex ERP replacement, or no sales owner |

These staff bands are targeting hypotheses. Qualification should use workflow complexity and economics, not headcount alone. Start with firms where a small improvement in won work or saved administration could plausibly cover the service cost. Ask the buyer for their own numbers; do not invent ROI.

## The three concrete offers

### 1. Ingenium Websites — campaign: “Make your next enquiry easier”

Offer a scoped website build with discovery, agreed page inventory, mobile layouts, copy guidance, service proof, tested enquiry forms, technical SEO foundations, analytics configuration and handover. Define who writes copy, supplies photography and approves legal content. Website-only customers can use their current enquiry process; CRM is an optional next step.

Entry offer: a 20-minute website enquiry review covering the offer, proof and contact path. Deliver a one-page scorecard and three prioritised fixes; avoid giving every prospect a free custom design. Qualify before preparing a large proposal.

Conversion asset: a real before/after service-page walkthrough showing clearer navigation, proof and next step. If no analytics baseline exists, label the improvement as a design or workflow change, not a measured uplift.

Landing page draft:

> **A website that makes it easier to choose your business.**
>
> Show what you do, give customers reasons to trust you, and make the next step clear. Ingenium builds websites around the questions your customers need answered before they enquire.
>
> Start with a clear scope, agreed milestones and a plan for keeping your site useful after launch.
>
> **Book a website enquiry review**

Three supporting sections: “Explain your services”; “Put your best work where buyers can see it”; “Make enquiries straightforward”. Add scope, indicative delivery dependencies, approved project examples, ownership/hosting explanation and one CTA.

Search ad draft headlines: “Websites for Irish SMEs”; “Make Enquiries Easier”; “Book a Website Review”. Description: “Show your services clearly and help customers take the next step. Discuss your website with Ingenium.” Use actual targeting settings to restrict served locations; do not manufacture location pages.

LinkedIn founder post draft:

> A useful website answers three questions before it asks for an enquiry: Can you solve my problem? Can I trust you? What happens next? Our website review follows that exact path. We look at one service, its proof and its enquiry form, then give you three practical improvements. If your site is due a rethink, book an Ingenium website enquiry review.

Opted-in launch email subject: “Does your website make the next step clear?” Body: “We are opening a new round of Ingenium website projects for businesses that need clearer services, stronger proof and a simpler enquiry path. Start with a short website review. We will identify three priorities and explain what a rebuild would involve. Book your review.” Only say a new round is opening when delivery slots are confirmed.

### 2. Ingenium CRM — campaign: “Know who owns the next step”

Offer process mapping followed by a paid, defined implementation: pipeline and fields, data cleanup/migration allowance, permissions, agreed routing rules, reporting, training and a post-launch adoption check. Separate three pathways: configure an existing platform, migrate to an appropriate platform, or build custom workflows when their economics justify it. Do not force a custom build when an existing CRM fits.

Entry offer: a 30-minute follow-up review. Ask the buyer to bring one anonymised opportunity from first enquiry to quote. Deliver a workflow sketch and a recommended next action. Paid discovery is appropriate once multiple systems, data migration or approval processes are involved.

Conversion asset: a five-minute demonstration of an overdue quote, its responsible person, next action and management view. Use synthetic records. To sell an adoption claim, collect actual user behaviour, not screenshots of an empty dashboard.

Landing page draft:

> **Every opportunity needs an owner and a next step.**
>
> Ingenium helps your team organise enquiries, quotes and follow-up in one usable CRM workflow. We map the process, configure or build the right system, and help the team put it to work.
>
> Keep your current website if it is doing its job. Start with the part of the process that needs attention.
>
> **Book a follow-up review**

Search ad draft headlines: “CRM Setup & Improvement”; “Organise Sales Follow-Up”; “Map Your CRM Workflow”. Description: “Bring clarity to enquiries, owners and next actions. Discuss CRM setup, migration or improvements with Ingenium.” Only target vendor-specific terms when Ingenium can genuinely deliver that vendor's work.

LinkedIn founder post draft:

> Open your last ten enquiries. For each one, can the team see who owns it, what happened and what happens next? That is the starting point for an Ingenium CRM project. We map the working process before deciding what software or configuration is needed. Our follow-up review shows where a shared pipeline could help and what should happen first.

Opted-in launch email subject: “Who owns the next step on your open quotes?” Body: “If the answer lives across several inboxes, a clearer process may help. Ingenium CRM projects start with how enquiries move through your business. We then scope the records, stages, responsibilities and reporting your team needs. Book a follow-up review and bring one example of a quote or enquiry that became difficult to track.”

### 3. Ingenium Connected — campaign: “From enquiry to next action”

This is the flagship: website and CRM designed together, with the agreed native lead flow included in the build scope. Proposed standard flow: form submission → contact/opportunity record → source and service context → assigned owner → acknowledgement/next task → visible pipeline/report. Include duplicate handling, failed-delivery visibility and a manual recovery process in acceptance testing.

Define “native” precisely in sales documents: which Ingenium-managed website and CRM components share the workflow and which connections remain external. Validate that this describes the actual product. Separate accounting, ERP, telephony, legacy CRMs, ecommerce inventory, custom migration and unusual bidirectional sync as scoped additions. “No separate integration project for the included website-to-CRM flow” is safer and more informative than “no integration costs”.

Entry offer: a 30-minute enquiry-to-sale walkthrough, with a live synthetic demo and one mapped buyer workflow. Paid blueprint follows when fit is established; credit its cost against implementation only if the commercial model supports this.

Landing page draft:

> **Your website and CRM, built to work together from day one.**
>
> When someone enquires, the details should reach the people who need them. Ingenium builds your website and CRM around the same process, so the agreed forms, lead records and follow-up steps work together at launch.
>
> The standard website-to-CRM connection is included in your scoped build. Any extra systems or migration work are agreed separately before work begins.
>
> **See an enquiry become a next step**

Search ad draft headlines: “Website + CRM Together”; “Connected From Day One”; “See the Enquiry Journey”. Description: “One scoped build for your website and CRM. See the included lead flow and discuss your requirements.”

LinkedIn founder post draft:

> A website launch should include what happens after someone presses Send. Here is the flow we design around: enquiry → contact record → service and source context → owner → next action. Ingenium Connected brings the website and CRM into the same project, with that agreed connection included. Watch the demo to see the handoff, then decide whether it fits how your team works.

Opted-in launch email subject: “What happens after someone presses Send?” Body: “Ingenium Connected brings your website and CRM into one scoped build. We design the enquiry form and the follow-up process together, so the agreed lead flow is ready at launch. See the demonstration, including which connections are included and how extra systems are scoped.”

Launch incentive for all lanes: a useful included review after launch, such as a 30-day website enquiry review, CRM adoption review, or connected-flow review. Confirm its hours and scope. Avoid permanent percentage discounts and invented scarcity. If capacity allows only three pilots, state three because that is the real capacity.

## Ecommerce extension

Target established specialist retailers with reliable product data and fulfilment, especially where customers both enquire and purchase. Offer a commerce discovery covering catalogue, variants, payments, shipping, tax settings, returns, stock and customer communication. Use the buyer's existing platform if appropriate. Keep platform subscriptions, gateway charges, catalogue preparation and integrations visible in the quote.

Campaign angle: “Make it easier to buy, and easier to look after the customer afterwards.” Entry CTA: “Review your buying journey.” Build a separate page once there is genuine ecommerce proof. A catalogue or sales enquiry website is not proof of completed ecommerce delivery. Do not position the initial combined package as a full inventory or order-management suite.

## Channel plan

Owned: three landing pages, a comparison/route selector, one clear booking form, permissioned email list, demonstration and case studies. Capture lane and original source in the CRM. A contact request and a marketing subscription should remain distinct choices.

Rented: founder LinkedIn for demonstrations and specific workflow lessons; small Google Search pilots for existing buying intent. Start with website-rebuild and CRM-implementation intent in separate campaigns. The combined offer can be introduced on these landing pages and through remarketing where permitted; do not assume buyers already search for Ingenium's category wording. Avoid paid LinkedIn initially unless deal economics justify a narrow account list.

Borrowed: seek introductions through accountants, IT support providers, brand designers and marketing specialists whose clients have these problems. Propose a practical workshop to an appropriate local chamber. LEO education and signposting may be relevant; verify supplier/application requirements before claiming any programme role. Topic: “Trace one enquiry: website, inbox, quote, follow-up.” Chambers provide a discoverable network, not an assured distribution contract.

Founder activity assumption per week: two useful posts, five requested/warm referral conversations, two tailored demonstrations, one partner conversation. Personalise account research without bulk unsolicited outreach. For electronic campaigns, validate the lawful recipient basis and include an easy opt-out; Irish DPC guidance sets consent as the general rule with limited exceptions. [DPC guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/rules-electronic-and-direct-marketing)

| Days 15–28 | Conduct warm/referral reviews; recruit up to 2–3 paid pilot projects within capacity; publish website page and one approved case story; collect objections and refine proposals | Founder sales | At least five substantive buyer conversations per leading lane before declaring message fit |
| Days 29–42 | Launch website campaign; publish proof walkthrough; introduce CRM campaign one week later; hold first workflow clinic; begin small search test | Marketing owner + founder | Tracking reconciles to actual qualified meetings; fulfilment workload remains manageable |
| Days 43–60 | Launch Connected campaign with demonstration; publish first verified workflow evidence; send opted-in lane-specific sequence; review search terms and partner pipeline | Founder + marketing | A prospect can understand included connection, exclusions, cost and ownership without a long explanation |
| Days 61–75 | Run second workshop; publish actual pilot learning; improve winning page; stop weak ad groups; ask satisfied clients for permission to use feedback | Marketing + delivery | Qualified pipeline and gross contribution justify continuation |
| Days 76–90 | Review sales and delivery cohorts; compare lanes on contribution and support load; expand one vertical/lane; decide whether ecommerce deserves a separate pilot | Founder | Written decision to scale, revise or stop each lane |

If starting 28 September, the 90-day window ends in late December. Prioritise selling and implementation scoping before mid-December, then use the quieter holiday period for follow-up by agreement, measurement and January scheduling. Do not force complex client go-lives through staff holidays.

Suggested content rhythm: Monday one specific problem; Wednesday a practical demonstration; Friday a useful case observation or answer to an objection. Rotate by launch phase rather than publishing three generic brand posts every day. Example sequence: “What a service page must explain”; “Ten enquiries, ten next actions”; “Watch the form create the record”; “What is included in a website-to-CRM connection”; “When you should keep your current CRM”; “What a 30-day adoption review checks”.

Lean planning envelope agreed with the parent workstream: €1,500/month for three months (€4,500 total), excluding founder labour and VAT, with no more than three concurrent projects until capacity is validated. Allocate provisionally €750 to search tests, €300 to proof/demo production, €250 to local partner/event access and €200 to measurement/tools or contingency each month. These are allowances, not verified supplier quotes. Hold ad spend in reserve during the first two weeks while proof is repaired. Then run web and CRM intent campaigns sequentially rather than dividing tiny budgets across all three lanes. Reassign the next month's budget on qualified opportunity quality and contribution, not click volume.

## Measurement, qualification and operating rules

Minimal form: name, business email, company/site, main need (website/CRM/both/ecommerce/not sure), short problem statement. Ask timeline and budget band during booking if form friction is a concern. Internal lead record: lane, vertical, source/UTM where available, problem, process owner, fit, next action, opportunity value, expected close, lost reason and permission status. Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive data.

Discovery questions: What event triggered this project? Where do enquiries arrive? Who follows up? How many people touch an opportunity? What does a typical sale contribute? Which systems must stay? What data would move? Who approves content and process? What must be true at launch? Who signs off and when? Route website-only buyers without pressuring a CRM upsell.

Pipeline: enquiry → qualified review → discovery complete → scoped proposal → agreed/paid → in delivery → live → 30-day review. Record disqualified separately. Qualify on a real pain, an owner, viable scope, timing and plausible budget. A meeting is not a qualified opportunity unless these are understood.

Proposed day-90 learning target: 24 completed reviews across all channels, 12 qualified opportunities, 6 scoped proposals, 3 signed projects, subject to delivery capacity. This is an experiment target, not a forecast. Count contribution from upfront delivery and recurring services separately; do not count multi-year contract face value as collected revenue.

Dashboard by lane: qualified opportunities, held meetings, proposal-to-win rate, cash collected, acquisition cost including sales time, expected delivery gross margin, actual hours, time to launch, support load, payment failures and cancellations. For clients, track website qualified enquiries, CRM ownership/next-action completeness, and combined capture-to-first-human-action time. Record baseline and sample size before claiming improvement.

Ad-budget rule: set affordable acquisition cost from first-year expected gross contribution, then choose a conservative allowable share. As a planning rule, review an ad group if it spends the target acquisition cost without a qualified opportunity; pause or revise by twice that amount unless there is a documented reason to continue. These are management thresholds, not statistically conclusive results. Do not declare a winner from a handful of clicks. Use interviews and sales objections alongside small-volume data.

Do not test weekly versus monthly pricing at the same time as entirely different packages, prices and audiences. Hold offer and annual cost consistent when testing price framing; separately test actual billing cadence and cash-collection effects. Record qualified conversion, gross contribution, retention and failed payments, not just cheap lead volume. Final cadence and prices belong in the pricing workstream.

## Proof required before scaling

1. At least one permissioned, working case page per promoted capability; distinguish a website case from CRM or ecommerce delivery.
2. A synthetic end-to-end demo showing the included connected flow, duplicate/failure handling and what a human does next.
3. Scope sheet listing limits, client dependencies, integrations, licensing, support, ownership, export and exit arrangements.
4. A delivery checklist with named responsibility and testable acceptance criteria.
5. A measured case only after obtaining a baseline, observation period, denominator and permission. Use descriptive proof until then.
6. A weekly capacity view. Stop adding campaign volume before delivery quality or support response becomes unreliable.

The best first launch is a credible small implementation cohort whose evidence makes the second launch stronger. Paid pilots, clear outcomes and a useful 30-day review are more appropriate here than a broad free SaaS beta or a Product Hunt campaign.
