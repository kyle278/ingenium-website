# Ingenium pricing research and commercial model

Research observed 26 September 2026. Irish SME market; UK comparisons retain GBP. Public advertised prices are offers, not evidence of achieved selling prices, market averages, profitability or conversion. Recommendations and cost assumptions below are hypotheses for validation, not Ingenium's historical results. All proposed Ingenium prices exclude VAT. Competitors' VAT treatment is stated only where observed.

## Recommendation

Launch with a fixed implementation fee plus monthly care/platform service. Make websites, CRM configuration and a connected website/CRM three clear buying routes, with ecommerce a scoped website variant. Use monthly billing as the default; test a transparent weekly equivalent as secondary price framing. Do not fund complex bespoke builds through cheap, cancellable weekly subscriptions.

The competitive opportunity is a repeatable, accountable website-to-sales process with visible scope and total cost. Native connection can reduce duplicate setup and connector work, but does not eliminate process mapping, migration, permission design, training or third-party integration costs. A connection included in the package is a defensible promise only for the supported, tested workflow.

## Does cheaper weekly pricing outperform monthly?

**Evidence supports testing smaller-period framing; it does not establish that weekly billing improves an Irish agency's retained profit.** Four things commonly get mixed together: the displayed number, the amount actually collected, the interval between collections, and the contractual commitment.

### Evidence and limitations

1. [Gourville, 1998, Journal of Consumer Research](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/24/4/395/1797969) reports laboratory evidence that presenting costs in small recurring units can improve transaction evaluation even when payments remain aggregated. The mechanism concerns comparison standards, not proof that a weekly payment schedule is superior. Publisher abstract reviewed; full experimental tables were not accessible.
2. [Gourville, 2003, Marketing Letters](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1025467002310) reports that the effect generalises beyond daily framing but can reverse as the monetary magnitude increases. A smaller time unit is therefore not universally persuasive, particularly when moving from inexpensive consumer purchases to business services. Publisher abstract reviewed; do not infer an agency conversion uplift from it.
3. [Hamburger Abendblatt's first-person experiment report, June 2026](https://www.inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hamburger-abendblatt-unlocked-a-20-subscription-uplift-with-weekly-price-framing) describes an October 2025 test: 363 subscriptions in control versus 437 in variant, a reported 20% lift. Control was €9.90/month; variant displayed €2.50/week but billed every four weeks. Annualised spend rose from €118.80 to €130. It changed both price and billing interval, despite describing framing as the change. Counts and a claimed 1% significance level are supplied, but exposure denominators are not; significance cannot be independently reproduced from that article. Retention remained a future investigation. This is relevant field evidence for a package of changes in news subscriptions, not proof for agency services.
4. [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps) reports median year-one retention around 1–2% for weekly subscriptions, 6–14% monthly and 20–40% yearly across categories. Its selected app population, product differences and customer self-selection prevent causal interpretation. A prepaid annual subscriber's retained access also differs mechanically from repeated weekly renewal. Still, it directly challenges the idea that more initial weekly purchases necessarily yield durable recurring revenue. These mobile-app rates must not become Ingenium's forecasts.

### Price arithmetic that must appear correctly

| Offer | Recurring annual equivalent | Average monthly equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| €49/week, 52 charges | €2,548 | €212.33 |
| €199/calendar month, 12 charges | €2,388 | €199 |
| €49 every four weeks, 13 charges | €637 | €53.08 |
| €149/month, displayed as a weekly equivalent | €1,788 | €149; approximately €34.38/week |

Weekly figures use a 52-week comparison convention; an actual calendar-year charge schedule can differ. Never multiply a weekly charge by four and call that the monthly equivalent. A four-week invoice is not a calendar-month invoice. A low weekly number can cost more per year, and should never imply weekly cancellation if there is a 12-month commitment.

**Suggested test copy:** “€149/month + VAT, billed monthly. Approximately €34.38/week equivalent. €1,500 setup; first 12 months €3,288 + VAT.” Setup must remain visible beside the recurring fee. For the full-build-payment route, monthly care can be cancellable on a defined notice period; show that condition separately from the implementation agreement.

These are repeatable configurations, not bespoke platform development. Product readiness must be verified before publishing features: the parent review found the production automation runtime disabled. Up to three automation rules below are proposed delivery scope conditional on release, successful acceptance testing and explicit inclusion in the signed quote; do not advertise them as live today.

Website and CRM bought separately total €4,500 setup plus €398/month. Connected saves €49/month against that proposed Ingenium sum, or €588 over 12 months. It does not include a setup discount and does not claim savings against every competitor. Combine only where shared support and infrastructure really avoid duplicate effort.

Excluded from starter scope: content strategy or full copywriting, rebranding, historical email migration, data cleansing, bespoke objects/permissions, complex reporting, external APIs, accounting/ERP integrations, SMS/WhatsApp/email usage, AI credits, complex ecommerce variants/subscriptions, international tax configuration, and ongoing SEO campaigns. Define overages before work: initially €95/hour ex VAT with written estimate, or fixed-price change order; no unlimited support promise. Included support time does not roll over. Define business-hours response targets without promising 24/7 engineering.

For bespoke websites or CRM, sell a €750 discovery workshop producing process map, scope, dependencies, acceptance criteria and fixed-price proposal, credited in full against the approved implementation. Do not double count that credit as extra revenue in the model. Directional internal budgeting bands: complex websites €3,000–€8,000+, custom CRM €6,000–€15,000+, connected bespoke projects €8,000–€20,000+; these are scope-dependent planning hypotheses, not public fixed offers or market averages. Significant enterprise requirements may exceed them substantially.

### Payment and ownership model

- Default implementation: 50% on agreed scope, 50% at acceptance; monthly service starts at launch. Set objective acceptance tests and a client-content deadline. Avoid indefinite delivery financed by a small deposit.
- Website cash-flow alternative: €499 setup plus €249/month for 12 months, then €149/month. First year €3,487; disclose that the first-year fee contains €100/month of build repayment. This is €199 more than the standard route over 12 months. Do not call it cancel-anytime. Scope is identical to Website Launch, and price/credit/default terms need a suitable written business contract.
- Optional annual prepay: consider only after real cost data; a 5% discount on the service component is a test hypothesis, not a default 20% SaaS-style discount applied to labour.
- Client keeps domain registration, business content and customer data. On the fully paid build route, transfer agreed custom site deliverables with final payment. Third-party software and Ingenium shared platform IP remain licensed, not sold outright. State source-code rights separately for each architecture.
- Define export formats for contacts, deals and content; named export assistance allowance; cancellation notice; retention/deletion schedule; outstanding build balance on instalment exit; and whether continued platform subscription is necessary to run the CRM. Do not promise a portable standalone CRM if it depends on Ingenium's service. A customer data export and ownership of application software are different things.

## Unit economics: worked planning model

All values below are illustrative, ex VAT, with 12 full paying service months and no churn/default. They are neither guaranteed margins nor Ingenium's actual costs. The loaded delivery rate is €45/hour, including allocated delivery overhead once. Build hours include scoping, design/configuration, QA, revisions and training. Monthly support hours include client contact/admin for service delivery; no separate duplicate support allowance. Platform reserves cover vendor infrastructure only. CAC includes sales/marketing work and spend, separate from delivery labour. Shared core product R&D and corporate overhead not represented in the loaded rate must still be covered from contribution.

Formula: revenue = setup + 12 × monthly fee. Contribution = revenue − build hours × hourly cost − 12 × (support hours × hourly cost + platform reserve) − CAC − payment fees. It is contribution after acquisition, not net profit.

| Assumption/result | Website | CRM | Connected | Ecommerce |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Build hours | 20 | 35 | 50 | 40 |
| Support hours/month | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1.5 |
| Infrastructure reserve/month | €20 | €35 | €45 | €20 |
| CAC per won account | €350 | €650 | €750 | €500 |
| First-year revenue | €3,288 | €5,988 | €8,688 | €5,888 |
| Total modelled costs | €2,082.57 | €3,818.07 | €5,293.57 | €3,441.57 |
| Contribution after CAC | €1,205.43 | €2,169.93 | €3,394.43 | €2,446.43 |
| Contribution margin | 36.7% | 36.2% | 39.1% | 41.5% |
| Margin with all delivery hours +25% | 25.7% | 25.2% | 27.9% | 30.5% |
| Monthly service contribution before CAC/R&D | 54.7% | 48.2% | 46.9% | 54.4% |

Fees use an illustrative single setup payment and 12 monthly card charges at the [Stripe Ireland](https://stripe.com/ie/pricing) standard EEA card rate, 1.5% + €0.25. Two implementation instalments add another €0.25 in this simplified calculation. Premium/international cards, fees on VAT collected, Stripe Billing (listed separately at 0.7% of billing volume), disputes and bad debt are not included; add the actual stack before approving a quote. A recurring Billing fee would reduce service margin by another 0.7 percentage points on the affected revenue. External CRM and ecommerce licences are client-paid; if Ingenium absorbs them, subtract their full cost.

At €2,388 annual recurring revenue, 12 card charges cost €38.82 and 52 equivalent-value charges €48.82: €10/year more in fixed fees. Fees alone are a small issue; collections, support burden, credit exposure and retention matter more.

**Pricing gates:** initially require at least 35% contribution after CAC in the base year-one case, at least 25% with a 25% labour overrun, and at least 45% recurring service contribution before shared product R&D. These are management thresholds, not industry benchmarks. The CRM overrun case is particularly thin. Charge more, narrow scope, or refuse the standard package when a gate fails. Track real hours by activity for the first five accounts in each lane. Never treat unpaid founder labour as free.

Connected at €3,900 setup was rejected: it produced only 34.7% base and 22.7% stressed contribution in this model. At €4,500 it clears the provisional gates. Even the adopted price cannot fund unlimited engineering or large platform licence costs.

The Website cash-flow alternative has €1,250 initial build-plus-CAC cost against €499 cash collected before fees. Monthly €249 less €65 service cost and approximately €3.99 card fees gives about €180 contribution toward the shortfall: approximately five paying months recover the initial gap. The contract does not make defaults disappear; cap financed launches until collections are proven. The standard upfront fee already covers modelled build plus CAC, reducing cash exposure.

For value discussions use the customer's contribution per additional sale, not their sales revenue. At €349 monthly recurring cost and €300 contribution per incremental won job, approximately 1.17 additional jobs/month cover the recurring fee alone. Including €4,500 setup spread over 12 months raises the first-year equivalent to €724/month, or 2.41 jobs. These are break-even scenarios, not promised lift or proof of attribution.

## A credible test programme

1. Interview 12–15 owners across the three lanes before public pricing is fixed. Ask what their last site/CRM cost, where handoffs fail, what they pay now, who approves purchases, how they budget, and what outcome makes a project worth paying for. Then show an identical written scope and ask too cheap, good value, expensive but possible, and too expensive. Use ranges qualitatively at this sample size; do not call the crossings statistically optimal prices.
2. Deliver 3–5 scoped pilots per lane where capacity allows; collect actual delivery hours, revision time, licence costs, time to activation and payment collection. These operational pilots test feasibility, not market-wide conversion superiority.
3. Run a clean **framing-only** test for one sufficiently trafficked lane. Control leads with “€149/month”; variant leads with “approximately €34.38/week equivalent, billed €149/month”. Both show identical setup, total cost, service, cancellation and CTA. Keep actual billing monthly. Randomise 50/50 persistently at account/visitor level, exclude staff/bots, and avoid different variants reaching the same company through different people where identifiable. Keep acquisition channel mix stable and record device/channel.
4. Pre-register qualified lead definition and primary conversion to signed, paid, in-scope customer within a fixed 30-day conversion window. Monitor lead-to-meeting and proposal win rate as diagnostics, not substitute wins. Commercial decision metric: collected contribution per assigned eligible visitor/account, plus 90-day contribution, support demand, refunds/defaults and cancellation. Preserve original cohort assignment and measure intent-to-treat.
5. Illustrative sample sizing: detecting 5% versus 7% conversion needs roughly 2,200 independent observations per arm at two-sided 5% significance and 80% power. Detecting 20% versus 30% at the qualified-opportunity stage needs roughly 300 per arm. These are approximate binomial planning calculations, not Ingenium baseline estimates. Use real baseline conversion and a commercially useful minimum effect before launch. Low traffic means interview and sales evidence will guide decisions; 20 proposals cannot establish a modest uplift.
6. Run to the predetermined sample and full conversion window, spanning full business weeks. Do not stop after a favourable week or declare victory from one lane then apply it universally. If sample is unattainable in 90 days, report inconclusive and retain monthly default.
7. Only after framing evidence, test actual weekly versus monthly collection separately with equivalent annual cost, same commitment and same deliverables. Then, separately, test commitment length or price. Track total cash and contribution over 90–180 days; weekly-to-monthly MRR normalisation alone can hide early churn. Do not simultaneously lower setup, change billing and rewrite the offer and then attribute the result solely to weekly cadence.

Approve a winning variant only if incremental contribution clears the margin gates without worsening comprehension of total cost or ownership. Ask a small subset of buyers to restate their setup fee, monthly invoice and minimum commitment: apparent conversion uplift caused by misunderstanding is not sustainable positioning.

## Evidence limits and decisions needed

No Ingenium delivery time logs, historical close rates, churn, CAC, capacity or willingness-to-pay survey were available to this research subtask. No controlled Irish B2B web-agency weekly-versus-monthly billing study was identified. The strongest directly relevant experiment is consumer publishing and bundles multiple changes. Proposed prices should therefore be used as explicit launch hypotheses with scope and margin gates, not described as scientifically optimal prices.

The model's €350–€750 CAC assumes an efficient acquisition process; it is not the launch campaign forecast. For example, €1,500/month cash marketing over three months plus 10 founder sales hours/week for 13 weeks at €45/hour costs €10,350. Three wins imply €3,450 fully loaded CAC each, which fails the assumed acquisition economics. Separate one-time learning expenditure from repeatable CAC but report both transparently; stage paid-media spend and avoid presenting a small pilot cohort as profitable scalable growth.

Before publication verify the supported CRM user/record limits, release state of native automations, actual infrastructure costs, tax presentation, export capability and exact ownership agreement. Those checks determine whether a low recurring fee is commercially viable and whether the connected-product promise is true.
